How it is computed

Simple heuristic: a daytime MUF is estimated from F10.7 (<70→13 MHz, <90→17, <110→21, <130→24, <150→27, ≥150→30); at night 45% of that is used. On 14, 21 and 28 MHz the frequency is compared with the MUF (≤90% good, ≤110% fair, higher poor). On 80 m and 40 m: good at night; by day 40 m fair and 80 m poor (D-layer absorption). Kp ≥ 4 lowers one level and Kp ≥ 5 two levels (one on 80/40 m). It ignores season, latitude, exact local time and path direction.

Manual values (optional)

If NOAA does not respond you can type F10.7 and Kp to drive the table. Empty = use downloaded data.

Scales and meaning

K index (0–9): geomagnetic field disturbance over 3 h

  • Quiet (Kp < 3)
  • Unsettled (Kp 3)
  • Active (Kp 4)
  • Minor storm G1 (Kp 5)
  • Moderate storm G2 (Kp 6)
  • Strong storm G3 (Kp 7)
  • Severe storm G4 (Kp 8)
  • Extreme storm G5 (Kp 9)

A index: daily-equivalent average (0–400)

  • Quiet (0–7)
  • Unsettled (8–15)
  • Active (16–29)
  • Minor storm (30–49)
  • Major storm (50–99)
  • Severe storm (100+)

Solar flux F10.7: higher = more ionization = higher bands more open

  • Very low (<70): low/mid bands only
  • Low (70–89): 15 m marginal, 10 m rarely
  • Moderate (90–119): 15 m good, 10 m at times
  • Good (120–149): 10 m opens regularly
  • High (≥150): 10 m and 12 m very active

Sustained negative (southward) Bz below about −5 nT favors geomagnetic storms and degrades HF, especially at high latitudes.

Source: NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center · swpc.noaa.gov
